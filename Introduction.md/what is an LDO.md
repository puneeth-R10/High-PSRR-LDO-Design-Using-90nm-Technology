A Low Dropout Regulator (LDO) is a fundamental analog building block widely used in modern electronic systems to provide a stable and noise-free output voltage despite variations in input supply and load conditions. In wearable devices—such as fitness trackers, smartwatches, and biomedical sensors—power management becomes critically important due to strict constraints on battery life, compact size, and continuous operation. These systems often integrate sensitive analog and mixed-signal circuits like ADCs, RF modules, and bio signal amplifiers, all of which demand a clean and stable supply. The LDO plays a vital role in ensuring voltage regulation with minimal ripple, low dropout voltage, and high efficiency, thereby directly impacting the reliability and longevity of wearable electronics.

Wearable health monitoring devices such as ECG patches, smart watches, pulse monitoring nodes, glucose sensors and portable diagnostic modules depend on a reliable power management unit. The analog front-end in these systems must process microvolt- and millivolt-level biological signals, so supply ripple can directly appear as measurement error, baseline drift or false detection. LDO regulators are therefore used after batteries or switching converters to provide a quiet local supply for sensitive analog, mixed-signal and RF blocks.

An LDO is preferred in these applications because it is simpler and quieter than a switching converter, operates with a small input-output voltage headroom and offers good output accuracy when the feedback loop is properly designed. The price paid for this simplicity is that PSRR, dropout voltage, transient response and stability become strongly dependent on the pass transistor, error amplifier gain, compensation network and load conditions [6], [11].
The present project focuses on a high-PSRR LDO implemented in Cadence Virtuoso. The goal is not only to obtain a regulated output voltage, but to demonstrate measurable improvement in line regulation, load regulation, PSRR, loop stability and transient behavior. This makes the work suitable for wearable health monitoring devices where power integrity and long battery life must be considered together.

For the mobile and internet-of-things (IoT) applications, the compact integration and low power dissipation is necessary in chip design. System-on-chip (SoC) that is contained every function in a compact chip become popular since it can remove chip-to-chip path-caused parasitic by removing power and signal delivery between chips. According this tendency, power management integrated circuits (PMICs) should be fully-integrated and thus more complex strategies are needed when designing these circuits.

A battery provides charges to the inputs of DC/DC converters and these converters deliver supply voltages to loads containing charges in capacitors (CL,DC/DCs) as the level of desired voltage. However, these supplies contain large switching ripples by the operation properties of these converters and spurs from other loads sharing same supply voltage. These problems make the loads which need clean and noise-less supply cannot operate properly. Furthermore, the loads can operate optimally in their own supply voltage level but DC/DC converters only provide same voltage level to their loads.

To resolve these problems, low-dropout voltage regulators (LDOs) are added between switching converters and loads as shown in the bottom of Figure 1. Even if the LDOs have lower power efficiency than DC/DC converters due to series connection on current provision path, they can suppress switching ripples from DC/DC converters, reduce spurs from other loads, and provide different supply voltage to the different loads.
